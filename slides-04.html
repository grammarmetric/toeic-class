Warm-up · Unit 4 — Listening Part 4: Talks Lessons

Five-minute warm-up

One voice.
Nobody to interrupt it.

Listening Part 4 · Talks

The last listening part — and the one where the answer goes past you if you are still writing.

What happens

A single speaker talks for about forty seconds.

You hear

An announcement, a message, an advert, a briefing.

You read

Three printed questions with four choices each.

The catch

The three answers arrive in order, spread through the talk.

There is no second speaker to repeat the point. Whatever you miss is gone.

Where you meet them

Six situations cover almost every Part 4 talk.

Station / airport

Delays, gate changes, replacement services

Voicemail

A message you have to act on

Advertisement

A place or product being sold to you

Staff briefing

A deadline, a change of plan, who does what

Public notice

Rules, collection days, safety advice

Tour / news

Facts about a place or an event

Guessing which of the six it is, before it starts, is worth several marks.

Worked example — choices only

What kind of talk is coming?

  • (A) At a supermarket  (B) At a library  (C) At a museum  (D) At a cinema
  • (A) A closing time has changed  (B) An exhibit is closed  (C) A card has expired  (D) A film is sold out
  • (A) Return to the entrance  (B) Speak to a guide  (C) Come back tomorrow  (D) Use the side stairs

A public announcement, indoors, telling visitors that something is shut and what to do instead. You have not heard a word yet — and you already know what to listen for.

Answer early

Mark it the moment you hear it. Do not wait.

What most students do

Listen to the whole talk, then try to remember three answers.

What works

Answer 1 during the first third, answer 2 in the middle, answer 3 near the end — then read ahead.

The voice will announce the questions afterwards. By then you should already be reading the next set.

The paraphrase trap

The choice is a translation of what you hear.

You hear

“Refreshments will be available in the foyer.”

You read

Snacks and drinks are provided.

You hear

“The talk has been postponed until Friday.”

You read

The event was moved to a later date.

You hear

“Two positions are currently vacant.”

You read

The company is seeking staff.

So the words in the choices are your starting point, not the words to listen for.

Your turn — out loud

Say it another way.

  • “Please refrain from using mobile phones.”
  • “The 4:20 service will terminate at Fielding.”
  • “All remaining stock has been reduced by half.”

Don’t use the phone. The train stops early — it doesn’t go all the way. Everything left is half price.

Now the book

Unit 4 · Talks

Paraphrasing an announcement, predicting the situation from the choices, twelve questions at test speed, then making announcements of your own.

Open the lesson →
Say: Fourth and last listening part. Ask what is different from yesterday — the answer you want is “only one person is speaking”.
Stress “in order”. Question 1’s answer comes early, question 3’s comes late. That is what makes reading along possible.
Round the group. Name a situation, they name a phrase they’d expect to hear. Thirty seconds, no more.
Take guesses before revealing. Someone will say cinema — good, ask what in the choices rules it out (“exhibit”, “guide”).
This is the day’s main habit. Say it plainly: if you are still deciding when the voice reads the questions, you are already behind.
One press at a time. Ask for their paraphrase before revealing the right-hand box. Accept anything close.
Oral only, fast. Do not write. If a student gives a word-for-word repeat, push for the everyday version.
