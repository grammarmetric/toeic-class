The seven, in one screen

1 · Photographs

Predict nouns and verbs from the photo first.

2 · Question–Response

Catch the question word. A repeated word is a trap.

3 · Conversations

Skim the questions; predict who, where, what about.

4 · Talks

Answer the moment you hear it, then read ahead.

5 · Incomplete Sentences

Part of speech first, then two passes.

6 · Text Completion

The tense is in the context, not the gap.

7 · Reading

Questions first; specific information first; NOT last.

Look at it now. It comes off the screen before you start.

Two habits underneath all seven

Prepare before. Let go after.

Prepare before

Every single part gives you time before the thing you are tested on. Photo, questions, choices, gaps — look first.

Let go after

A question you are still thinking about costs you the next one too. Guess, mark it, move.

If you remember nothing else from two weeks, remember these two.

How today runs

Listening · 32 questions

Four recordings, about twelve minutes of audio. One play. No pausing.

Reading · 20 questions

Twenty minutes on the clock for Parts 5, 6 and 7 together.

Then

We mark it together and you keep the page.

The reading clock does not stop you and nothing is submitted. It is there to make you feel the pace.

What a good score looks like

60%on a part you met a week ago is a pass. It means the tactic survived the week.

A part below half is not a disaster — it is the one we start with on Monday. Be honest with yourself while you mark.

Open the checkpoint →
Say: nothing new today. Take the pressure off — then put a little back: this is the day they find out what actually stuck.
Frame it as diagnosis, not judgment. Say plainly that you will use the results to choose what to reteach.
Give them fifteen seconds to read it, then move on. Do not talk through all seven — they answer them on the page in a moment.
This is the slide worth the five minutes. Ask for an example of each from their own experience this fortnight before you reveal.
Housekeeping. Say clearly: one play, no pausing, no going back — otherwise the listening score means nothing.
