Listening Part 2 · Question–Response

This is the part with the least on the page — and the most to catch.

What happens

You hear a question, then three responses.

You hear

One question or statement.

Then

Three responses — (A) (B) (C). Only three, not four.

Then

About five seconds, and the next one starts.

Miss the first three words of the question and the whole item is gone. There is nothing to read back.

The one thing to catch

The question word decides everything.

Where

→ a place

When

→ a time

Why

→ a reason

Who

→ a person

How

→ a method

How much

→ an amount

If you catch only one word of the question, make it that one.

Three ways they trick you

“Where did you put the sales report?”

AYes, I reported it this morning.same word
BOn your desk.correct
CIt's a very good sport.similar sound

Hearing a word you recognise is a warning, not a clue. The right answer often repeats nothing at all.

The three types, named

A · Same word

The response repeats a word from the question, with a different meaning.

B · Related subject

It talks about the right topic but never answers the question.

C · Similar sound

report / sport · staff / stuff · know / no

You will label real examples as A, B or C in a few minutes.

One more thing

The correct answer often does not answer directly.

“Are you coming to the sales meeting?”
AYes, I am.expected…
BI'm still waiting for the figures.correct

Real people answer sideways. Do not reject an option just because it does not start with yes or no.

Your turn — out loud

What kind of answer are you waiting for?

  • “Who's taking the minutes today?”
  • “How did the presentation go?”
  • “When does the shipment arrive?”

A person. An opinion. A time. Decide that before the options start.

Unit 2 · Question–Response

Key words and distractor types, twelve questions at test speed, then a phone role play with a fax nobody can read.

Open the lesson →
Say: Second listening day. Lead with the shock — nothing at all is printed. Ask how that changes how they prepare.
Stress the five seconds. If they are still thinking about item 4 when item 5 starts, they lose both. Letting go of a question is a skill.
Drill this fast. Say a question word, they shout the answer type. Ten seconds, round the group, twice.
Reveal one at a time. Ask which they would pick before showing tags — some will pick (A). That is the lesson: recognising a word feels like understanding.
Reference slide. These three letters are used in the book activity, so make sure the names stick before you open it.
Ask for a “yes/no” answer first, then reveal (B). Most students find this the hardest habit to break.
Oral only. Do not accept a full answer — you only want the type. Then reveal.
