Five-minute warm-up

Most of these are not vocabulary questions.

Reading Part 5 · Incomplete Sentences

They look like vocabulary. They are usually grammar — and that makes them fast.

What happens

One sentence, one gap, four options.

Everything is printed

No audio. You control the pace completely.

You control the clock

Reading is one long block — spend too long here and Part 7 suffers.

This is the part where good students still lose marks: not because it is hard, but because it is slow.

The pattern

Look at the four options first.

The new software has significantly ……… our production speed.
Aimprove
Bimprovement
Cimproved
Dimprovingly

Four forms of one word. You do not need to know what improve means. You need to know which form fits.

So the question becomes…

“What kind of word goes in the gap?”

Aimprove — base verbnot after has
Bimprovement — nounwe need a verb
Cimproved — past participlehas + -ed ✓
Dimprovingly — adverbnot a real word

Decide the part of speech and three options disappear at once.

Free clues

Word endings tell you the type.

-ment, -tion, -ity, -er

noun — improvement, donation, ability, courier

-ly

adverb — efficiently, roughly, intently

-ful, -ive, -able, -ous

adjective — colorful, impressive, predictable

-ed, -ing, -s

verb form — improved, working, needs

The other half of the unit

2passes through the questions — never one slow pass
  • First pass: answer only what you see instantly. Skip anything slow.
  • Second pass: go back to what you skipped. Guess, and move on.

Never leave a blank. There is no penalty for a wrong answer on the TOEIC.

Your turn — out loud

Noun, verb, adjective or adverb?

“The manager spoke very ……… about the new plan.”

Adverb — it describes spoke. So look for the option ending in -ly, and ignore the other three.

You did not need to see the options at all.
