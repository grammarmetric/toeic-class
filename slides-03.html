Listening Part 3 · Conversations

For the first time this week, everything is printed — and reading it first is the whole tactic.

What happens

Two speakers, then three questions about them.

You hear

Four short lines between a man and a woman. Once.

You read

Three questions, four choices each — printed in front of you.

You get

About 35 seconds before the next conversation begins.

Those 35 seconds are not a rest. They are the next question’s preparation time.

The tactic

Skim → predict → then listen.

Who

Colleagues? A shop assistant and a customer?

Where

An office, a shop, a street, a station?

What about

A problem, a request, a plan, a complaint?

Ten to fifteen seconds per item. Underline the nouns and verbs, nothing else.

Worked example — read only the choices

Where are we, and who is talking?

  • (A) Cancel the booking  (B) Change the date  (C) Pay a deposit  (D) Call the manager
  • (A) The room is too small  (B) The date is fully booked  (C) The price has risen  (D) The projector is broken

A meeting room, a hotel or a venue. One speaker is booking something; the other works there. You have not heard a word yet.

Why the choices look unfamiliar

The right answer almost never uses the words you heard.

You hear

“We’re out of those at the moment.”

You read

The item is not in stock.

You hear

“Could you give me a hand with these?”

You read

He asks for assistance.

You hear

“Leave it with me.”

You read

She offers to deal with it.

So while you skim, say the choices to yourself in other words. That is what your ear will actually meet.

Today’s language

Asking for something without sounding rude.

Softer request

Would you mind …ing?
Do you think I could …?
Are you by any chance …?

Saying no politely

I’m afraid I can’t right now.
Sorry, I’m really busy — maybe Bob could help.

Note the grammar trap: Would you mind takes -ing, but Do you think I could takes the plain verb.

Your turn — out loud, no writing

Predict the context.

  • “What does the man ask the woman to do?”
  • “Why is the woman unable to help?”
  • “What will the man do next?”

Two colleagues. One needs help. The other is busy — so the first one will go and ask somebody else. Now you are listening for something.

Now the book

Unit 3 · Conversations

Paraphrasing, predicting the context, twelve questions at test speed, then making and refusing requests with a partner.

Open the lesson →
Say: Third listening day, and the first with everything printed. Ask what they think that changes — then tell them: it moves the work to before the recording.
Sell the 35 seconds. Most students use them to recover. Good students use them to read ahead. Say it twice.
Keep it to three words: who, where, what about. They will use exactly this in exercise 2.
Reveal after they guess. Take two or three guesses first — accept anything defensible. The point is that a guess is possible at all.
This is the hardest idea of the day. Do all three pairs, one press at a time, and ask them to produce the paraphrase before you show it.
Drill the grammar. “Would you mind …?” + -ing. Round the group once, fast, before they open the book — section C depends on it.
Oral only. Do not accept an answer to the questions — only a prediction of the situation. Then reveal.
